How to use this report

This report highlights White House and Rapid Response 47 posts on X that drew unusually high reply volume relative to likes. A high ratio can signal disagreement, correction, or controversy; it doesn’t prove why people replied.

Each entry can be expanded to see the post and its five highest-engagement direct replies. A reply keeps the replier’s words. Their photo sits under those words, a post they quoted is embedded below them, and a link to another page shows the preview X stored with it.

How these numbers are counted

The ratio is replies per 10 likes: reply count × 10 ÷ like count. Counts are X’s numbers on the post as of the latest stored pull.

Original and quote posts only. Reposts, reply posts, polls, and direct questions are excluded. A question inside a longer post is kept.

The 4.10 default cutoff selects approximately the same upper-tail share as the Truth Social report’s 2.50 cutoff: about 0.44% of eligible posts. The 3.38 floor similarly matches the Truth Social report’s 2.00 floor: about 0.98%.

X / Twitter user profile

X’s audience is much younger than Truth Social’s and still skews heavily male. A 2026 audience estimate puts 58% of X users below age 35, while Similarweb estimates that 68% of x.com visitors are male. Pew’s 2026 U.S. survey shows the same pattern: 31% of adults under 30 use X, compared with 7% of those 65 and older, and 23% of men use it versus 14% of women. X also reaches Black adults (25%) and English-speaking Asian adults (27%) at higher rates than White adults (16%), and urban adults (21%) more than rural adults (14%).

In 2026, Pew found that 19% of U.S. adults used X. Its overall political reach was nearly even: 20% of Republicans and Republican leaners used it, compared with 19% of Democrats and Democratic leaners. But among Americans who regularly got news on X in 2025, 56% were Republican or Republican-leaning and 42% were Democratic or Democratic-leaning. In another 2025 Pew survey, 31% of X users said the platform favored conservative views, 5% said it favored liberal views, 38% said it treated both equally and 26% weren’t sure. This describes the platform around these responses, not a direct survey of the specific people replying.
